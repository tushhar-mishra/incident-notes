## ⚡ Core: the 10-minute version

**The idea.** Roblox's backend (about 18,000 servers, 170,000 containers) relied on **Consul** for service discovery, health checks, session locking and a key-value store; **Nomad** (scheduling) and **Vault** (secrets) depended on it too. On 28 October 2021, Consul became unhealthy and **nothing could be scheduled, discovered or authenticated**. Two problems hid inside Consul: a recently enabled **streaming** feature caused **contention** under heavy load, and **BoltDB** (Consul's Raft log storage) hit a performance pathology. Roblox's **monitoring also depended on Consul**, so they were partly blind.

```
Consul (5 voters + 5 non-voters, Raft)  ◄── service discovery, health checks, locks, KV
      ▲ ▲ ▲
      │ │ └── Vault (secrets)     everything depends on Consul → Consul = single point of failure
      │ └──── Nomad (scheduling)
      └────── telemetry/monitoring  ← circular dependency: can't see Consul's health well
Problem 1: streaming feature (enabled day before on a busy service) → contention on Go channels
Problem 2: BoltDB freelist pathology → every Raft write very expensive → slow leaders
False leads: bad hardware, bigger hardware, too much load, snapshot reset
Fix: disable streaming, avoid slow leaders, rebuild caches, cold start, readmit players by DNS steering
Oct 28 → Oct 31: 73 hours
```

**Picture it.** A city where every service — ambulances, buses, deliveries, even the traffic cameras — gets directions from one central dispatcher. One day the dispatcher's notebook becomes painfully slow to write in. The whole city stops, and because the traffic cameras also ask the dispatcher what to film, nobody can easily see what's wrong.

**The one thing to know.** **Know your single points of failure, especially shared infrastructure, and never let your monitoring depend on them.** Also: new features in critical infrastructure deserve the same caution as application changes.

**You've got it if you can:** explain why replacing hardware didn't help, and why recovery took a long, staged cold start.

## Summary

From 28 to 31 October 2021, Roblox suffered a 73-hour outage after its Consul cluster — a dependency of service discovery, scheduling (Nomad), secrets (Vault) and many services — became unhealthy. The causes were contention in Consul's newly enabled streaming feature under high load and a BoltDB performance issue in Consul's Raft log storage; diagnosis was slowed by false leads and by telemetry that itself depended on Consul. Roblox restored Consul, rebuilt its caching system, cold-started the platform and readmitted players gradually.

## Impact

| Item | Detail (from Roblox's postmortem) |
|---|---|
| Duration | **73 hours** (28–31 October 2021) |
| Players | Online players dropped to 50% of normal by 16:35 PST on 28 Oct, then the platform was down; 100% of players given access at 16:45 on 31 Oct |
| Data | No user data loss or unauthorized access (a Consul snapshot reset risked a small loss of system config data, not user data) |
| Cost | **Not publicly disclosed** |

## How it happened

**Problem 1 — streaming:** Roblox had upgraded Consul (1.9 → 1.10) to use a new **streaming** feature. HashiCorp explained streaming "used fewer concurrency control elements (Go channels)" than the older long-polling mechanism, so under very high read and write load, contention on a single Go channel caused blocking during writes. The new 128-core, dual-socket servers made contention on shared resources worse (NUMA effects).

**Problem 2 — BoltDB:** Consul stores its Raft log in **BoltDB**, which never shrinks its file; freed pages are tracked in a "freelist". Roblox's workload "exposed a pathological performance issue in BoltDB that made freelist maintenance extremely expensive": a 4.2 GB log store held only 489 MB of data, and the freelist held nearly a million free page IDs written out on every Raft append → **slow leaders**. HashiCorp identified this cause in the days after the incident.

```
Write to Consul KV → Raft leader appends log → BoltDB writes data + entire huge freelist (slow)
Reads/writes via streaming → contention on one Go channel → blocking
→ Consul cluster unhealthy → Nomad can't schedule, Vault can't serve secrets, services can't discover each other
```

## Why it spread

- **Single point of failure:** "the system failed because Consul was a single point of failure, and Consul was not healthy."
- **Circular dependency:** "There was a circular dependency between our telemetry systems and Consul", so when Consul was unhealthy, they lacked the telemetry to diagnose it.
- **Two unrelated problems** at once made each look like something else; Roblox says diagnosing them was "largely responsible for the extended downtime."

## How they found it

- Initial signals: Vault degradation and one Consul server at high CPU.
- False leads: degraded hardware (replaced nodes; faster hardware didn't help and may have hurt), load (reduced usage; didn't fix it), state (snapshot reset; didn't fix it).
- Breakthrough: **perf reports and flame graphs** showed time in streaming code paths and kernel spin locks via the streaming subscription path → streaming disabled.
- BoltDB cause found afterwards with HashiCorp.

## What they did to fix it

1. **Disabled streaming** across all Consul systems.
2. **Prevented problematic (slow) leaders** from staying elected (workaround), later a BoltDB compaction approach.
3. Used **iptables** to block and slowly readmit internal traffic to Consul during resets.
4. **Rebuilt the caching system** by redeploying it — hit by stale scheduling data in Consul KV, a scheduler placing work on an unhealthy node it saw as "completely open", and a deploy tool designed for incremental changes rather than starting from zero.
5. **Cold start** of services at the right capacity.
6. **Readmitted players gradually** using **DNS steering** (random percentages, others sent to a maintenance page), checking database load, cache performance and stability at each step.

## Permanent fix (as listed by Roblox, status as of January 2022)

| Change | Pattern it addresses |
|---|---|
| **Telemetry no longer depends on the systems it monitors**; extended Consul/BoltDB telemetry and targeted alerts | Circular dependency |
| Building an **additional, geographically distinct data center**; work towards multiple availability zones | Single location |
| **Sharding Consul**: critical services in dedicated clusters; moving core KV data to better storage; deleting obsolete KV data | Single point of failure |
| Replacing BoltDB with **bbolt** (planned Q1 2022) | BoltDB pathology |
| Streaming to be reintroduced carefully only after a new implementation is tested | Risky new features |
| Cache deployment redesigned to start from a standing start; Nomad improvements for large-job turn-up; faster machine config changes | Cold-start ability |

## Follow-up questions

1. **Q: What was the real root cause?**
   A: A single shared dependency (Consul) used for nearly everything, which failed because of contention in the newly enabled streaming feature under high load and a BoltDB performance pathology. Contributing: telemetry depended on Consul, and recovery tooling couldn't cold-start cleanly.

2. **Q: How was it detected, and why did diagnosis take so long?**
   A: Vault degradation and high Consul CPU. Diagnosis took long because there were two unrelated problems, plausible false leads (hardware, load), and limited telemetry due to the circular dependency.

3. **Q: What did they check first?**
   A: Hardware (replaced a node), then load (reduced it), then state (snapshot reset) — reasonable first hypotheses that turned out wrong.

4. **Q: How did they stop the impact?**
   A: Disabling streaming and handling slow leaders restored Consul; then caches were rebuilt and services cold-started.

5. **Q: Why was readmitting players done gradually?**
   A: A cold system can't absorb full load at once (cold caches, databases, thundering herd). DNS steering let them add players in steps while watching key metrics.

6. **Q: What changed permanently?**
   A: Independent telemetry, an extra data center, Consul sharding, bbolt, careful reintroduction of streaming, and improved cold-start tooling.

7. **Q: How would you confirm the system is healthy before opening up?**
   A: Key latency metrics (Consul KV writes, DB load, cache hit rate) stable under test traffic, then increase admission in steps.

8. **Q: What if disabling streaming hadn't worked?**
   A: Continue isolating: move critical services to a fresh, minimal Consul cluster, or bypass Consul for critical discovery with static configuration.

9. **Q: How would you prevent this class of incident?**
   A: Remove single points of failure in shared infrastructure, separate monitoring, test infrastructure features under production-like load, and practise cold starts.

10. **Q: Why didn't bigger hardware help?**
    A: The bottleneck was contention, not raw capacity; more cores across two sockets increased contention on shared resources. Adding capacity only helps when the limit is capacity.
